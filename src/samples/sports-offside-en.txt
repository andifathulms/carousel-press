Offside, | Explained Properly
No, you're not offside just for standing there.
---
Standing there isn't an offence
The Law says it plainly: being in an offside position is not an offence by itself.
---
What "offside position" means
You're in the opponents' half, and part of your head, body or feet is nearer their goal line than the ball and the second-last opponent.
---
Arms don't count
Hands and arms are ignored, even the goalkeeper's. For offside, the arm starts at the bottom of the armpit.
---
Level is onside
Level with the second-last opponent, or with the last two, is not an offside position.
---
When it's penalised
Only if you get involved when a teammate plays or touches the ball: playing it, blocking an opponent, or gaining an advantage.
---
No offside from these
Straight from a goal kick, a throw-in or a corner kick. Free kicks aren't on the list.
---
The punishment
An indirect free kick where the offence happened, even if that's in the player's own half.
