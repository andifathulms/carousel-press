## Catatan Kaki Sejarah

### sejarah-kutai-id: Kesultanan Kutai pernah dihapus
- Afriandi & Ariyadi, *Jurnal Reformasi* 10(2), 2020: https://jurnal.unitri.ac.id/index.php/reformasi/article/download/1919/pdf (UU Darurat 3/1953, UU 27/1959, 21 Jan 1960 handover, 22 Sep 2001 coronation)
- Sarip, *Yupa: Historical Studies Journal* 4(2), 2020: https://jurnal.fkip.unmul.ac.id/index.php/yupa/article/download/264/184 (1635 conquest of Muara Kaman, "ing Martapura")
- kaltimkece.id (Sarip): founding by Aji Batara Agung Dewa Sakti, palace now Museum Mulawarman, Sultan Aji Muhammad Arifin since 15 Dec 2018
- Left out: the year of the move to Tenggarong (often given as 1782, not verified); founding dates are traditional (*Salasilah Kutai*), so the deck says "menurut naskah".

### sejarah-yupa-id: Prasasti Yupa
- Sarip 2020 (above): seven yupa, Sanskrit in early Pallava script, 1879 and 1940 finds, Kudungga → Aswawarman → Mulawarman, no date on the stones, estimates c. 400 / 425 / 450 CE, name "Kutai" not in the inscriptions
- kaltimkece.id, "Menelusuri riwayat derma 20 ribu sapi": the 20,000 cows and archaeologists' doubt about the figure
- Museum Nasional Indonesia holds the originals.

### sejarah-syarif-kasim-id: Sultan Syarif Kasim II
- Wahyuni, Agustono & Warjio, "Siak Masa Revolusi (1945–1949)", *Yupa* 4(1), 2020: https://jurnal.fkip.unmul.ac.id/index.php/yupa/article/download/243/163 (3 Mar 1915, schools, 28 Nov 1945 telegram, 1946 Medan → Aceh, RRI, Rp20,000 + jewels)
- Setneg, Penerima Gelar Pahlawan Nasional 1959–2017: Keppres 109/TK/1998
- Disputed: "13 juta gulden" is widely repeated but no primary source was found; DJKN Kemenkeu says 10,000 guilders. The deck says the exact amount is unclear.
- Left out: the year the airport was renamed (not verified).

### sejarah-baabullah-id: Sultan Baabullah
- Indonesia Baik (Kominfo), profile of Sultan Baabullah: 1570, the siege, Portuguese gone by 1575
- Kompas English, 11 Nov 2020: reign 1570–1583, clove trade
- Setneg, 2020 National Heroes: Keppres 117/TK/2020
- Hanna & Des Alwi, *Turbulent Times Past in Ternate and Tidore* (1990), via Wikipedia
- Disputed: the exact 1575 date (15 July vs late December), so the deck gives the year only. "72 pulau" is a title from François Valentijn (1724), not a count.
- Left out: the exact day and killer of Sultan Khairun (only medium-confidence sources).

### sejarah-bongaya-id: Perjanjian Bongaya
- Sagimun M.D., *Sultan Hasanudin Menentang VOC* (Depdikbud, 1975), Wikisource Bab 4–5: 18 Nov 1667, Arung Palakka, war resumes 12 Apr 1668, Somba Opu falls 24 Jun 1669
- Atlas of Mutual Heritage (Nationaal Archief/RCE): Fort Ujung Pandang built 1630s, renamed Fort Rotterdam
- Treaty terms (monopoly, other Europeans out, forts razed except Somba Opu): Bassett 1958 and Ormrod & Rommelse 2020, cited via Wikipedia (medium confidence)
- Disputed: "Ayam Jantan dari Timur" as a Dutch title, no contemporary source; the number of articles in the treaty (24, 29 or ~30) is left out.
- Added Oct 2026 at the owner's request, from Wikipedia "Hasanuddin of Gowa" (en, read 13 Oct 2026; it cites Andaya 1981, Stapel, Van Dam van Isselt 1906): conflict from 1616 (VOC hostage-taking, 15 Dutch sailors killed); 27 Apr 1659 demand that the VOC stop attacking Gowa's vassals and admit its spice monopoly broke God's laws; a coastal brick wall of nearly 11 km; the VOC exhausted its ammunition without breaching Somba Opu; a siege of about two and a half years, with disease leaving at one point only 250 European soldiers fit; Hasanuddin abrogated the treaty within months. Medium confidence (Wikipedia, owner-chosen); the brief normally treats Wikipedia as a lead only.
- Date conflict: Somba Opu's fall is 24 Jun 1669 in Sagimun but 12 Jun 1669 in Wikipedia's text, so the slide says "Juni 1669". Wikipedia's ~10,000 Bugis troops vs the deck's ~8,000 (Andaya): the deck keeps 8,000.

### sejarah-banten-surosowan-id: Surosowan 1808
- Faizal Arifin, *Agastya* 11(1), 2021, doi 10.25273/ajsp.v11i1.8000 (1,000 infantry and four cannon, loot, sultan to Ambon, Anyer ultimatum, 12 Nov 1808 letter, Du Puy killed 14 or 15 Nov, Wargadireja executed, 22 Nov 1808 annexation incl. Lampung)
- Sunandar, Saepudin & Safitri, *Al-Isnad* 5(1), 2024, doi 10.22515/isnad.v5i01.8602 (arrival at the Surosowan gate 21 Nov 1808, Meeuwenbaai labour and malaria, the Wargadireja and Anyer demands)
- T.S. Raffles, *The History of Java* (1817), Project Gutenberg #49843 (resident murdered, sultan "banished to Amboina"; 1813 resignation for a 10,000 Spanish dollar pension)
- Atlas of Mutual Heritage, "Surosowan fort" (Meeuwenbaai labour, ~1,000 attackers vs 3,000 defenders, demolition completed 1832)
- Historia (Risa Herdahita Putri, Dec 2024); Kompas.com (20 Mar 2023) (1813 abolition under Raffles)
- DJKN Kanwil Banten (25 Apr 2025) (destruction 1808–1832, bricks reused, cagar budaya SK Mendikbud 139/M/1998)
- Left out: the sultans' names (sources disagree), how Wargadireja died (shot/beheaded), the number of labourers demanded (1,000 or 1,500), whether the palace was burned in 1808 (one weak source), the "Aku jadi Sultan!" anecdote (Sandick 1893).

### sejarah-puputan-bayu-id: Puputan Bayu dan hari jadi Banyuwangi
- S. Margana, *Java's Last Frontier: The Struggle for Hegemony of Blambangan, c. 1763–1813*, PhD thesis Leiden 2007, https://hdl.handle.net/1887/12547, ch. 4–5 (1767 takeover, Wilis exiled 1768, Rempeg as Jagapati Aug 1771, 18 Dec 1771 ambush and Van Schaar killed, the 1772 siege, Jagapati's death 11 Jun 1772, Bayu falls 11 Oct 1772, captives killed, < 3,000 left, capital moved 21 Oct 1774, why 18 Dec was chosen)
- Antara Jatim (16 Dec 2018) (DPRD session 9 May 1995 chose 18 Dec by acclamation; commemoration at Rowo Bayu)
- detikJatim (Ardian Fanani, 7 Feb 2021 and 21 May 2022) (Rowo Bayu in Songgon; the popular "60.000" figure; the local story of Jagapati's death)
- Kompas.com (Widya Lestari Ningsih, 29 Jun 2021) (second source for "lebih dari 60.000")
- Left out: popular-only claims ("10.000 tentara VOC", "delapan ton emas", 65,000 starting population), the Perda number (Margana says the date was approved in 1998, Antara gives the DPRD session of 1995), graphic details.

### sejarah-trunajaya-id: Trunajaya
- M.C. Ricklefs, *A History of Modern Indonesia since c.1200*, 4th ed. 2008, ch. 7 (Plered falls late May–late June 1677, Amangkurat I's death and burial at Tegalwangi, the father murdered 1656, Kajoran, the 1675 Makassar pact, Gogodog 1676, the Feb 1677 VOC treaty, Kediri 25 Nov 1678, capture by Jonker end 1679, stabbed by Amangkurat II Jan 1680, van Goens' 5,000–6,000 figure)

#### Left out / disputed

- **Date of the raid.** Tirto, detik and Wikipedia give 3 July 1964. Sellato gives 18 July 1964 as the date of the "Tragedi Bultiken". Magenda dates the arrest order to 24 April 1964. The deck keeps the April order (Magenda) and the July raid (Tirto, detik, Rahmadi) as separate beats.
- **Date of the palace burning.** Sources give 18 July (Sellato, Rahmadi), 23–24 July (detik, which puts the Raja Muda's palace on 18 July) or 24 July (Avatara). The slide says the date differs between sources.
- **Number of victims.** About 30 (Magenda), 87 victims with 37 dead (*Koran Kaltim* 2013, via Tirto and Rahmadi), at least 58 (witness via Tirto), dozens of aristocrats plus hundreds of commoners (Datu Fajar via Bond). The slide lists all four.
- **Commander's name.** Magenda writes "Soeharjo". detik identifies him as Brigjen Suhario Padmodiwiryo ("Hario Kecik"), and Wikipedia also has "Soepardjo". The deck uses Magenda's spelling. The Kutai deck writes "Soehario" for the same Pangdam Mulawarman. Align the two if you want them consistent.
- **"The sultan disappeared."** Wikipedia and Tirto say the reigning sultan vanished. Left out: the last sultan, Djalaluddin, died in 1958 (Sellato, Rahmadi), so in 1964 there was no reigning sultan. Who was in the palace (a Raja Muda, per detik) and his name (Datu Mukemat in blogs and en.wikipedia) could not be confirmed from a stronger source.
- **Order from Brigjen Soepardjo, Lt Simatupang ordering the shooting, and police officer M. Ramli as the shooter.** Left out as too specific: they come from *Lentera Timur* or a 2023 popular book, via Tirto and detik only.
- **The PKI angle.** Ibnu Purna (1999, via Tirto) says rogue officers controlled by the PKI carried it out. Magenda calls Soeharjo a known leftist. Rahmadi lists this as one of four hypotheses. Liputan6 even blames "orang-orang PKI". Left out because it's a 1965-adjacent claim with no firm source. The deck says only that the charge was never proven, and adds Bond's evidence of real Malaysia contacts so that it doesn't give a one-sided version.
- **Whether Bulungan really sided with Malaysia.** Bond (2020) argues there is reason to believe some aristocrats did. Rahmadi and Pemprov Kaltara say the charge was never proven. Both views are on slide 11.
- **A sultan after 1964.** Sellato says a new sultan, Maulana al-Mamun, was inaugurated in 2013. Liputan6 says Al-Makmun was installed in Tawau in October 2002. In 2013 Tirto still wrote that he "should have become" Sultan Bulungan X, and in 2021 the Lembaga Adat chair said there was no sultanate structure. The sources conflict, so the deck says only that the heir lived in Tawau and that adat is kept by the Lembaga Adat. Unlike Kutai, there's no clear "sultan again" date.
- **Replica museum date.** c. 2000 (Sellato) or 1998 (detik). The slide says 1998–2000.
- **Oil date.** Avatara says 1902. Other accounts commonly put the first Tarakan oil around 1905–1906, so the slide says "awal 1900-an".
- **Founding.** The 1731 founding under Wira Amir (Rahmadi) is used. Kompas and Wikipedia also give a 16th-century line under Datuk Mencang (1555), which comes from tradition. Left out.
- **Reburial or memorial for the victims.** None found. Bond notes the bodies were lost at sea. The annual tahlil and ziarah at the sultans' graves at Masjid Kasimuddin (Antara, Oct 2026) honours the sultans and their kin, not specifically the 1964 victims. Left out to avoid implying a Bultiken memorial.
- **Wikipedia only.** The "Brawijaya 5-17" spelling; "30 executed" attributed to Soepardjo's order; Datu Mukemat weighted with stones and shot between Tarakan and Bunyu (en.wikipedia, uncited). All left out.

### filsafat-epikuros-keinginan-id
- Epikuros, *Letter to Menoeceus*, tr. R.D. Hicks (Wikisource "Letter to Menoeceus"):
  - 127: "of desires some are natural, others are groundless; and of the natural some are necessary as well as natural, and some natural only".
  - 131: "we do not mean the pleasures of the prodigal or the pleasures of sensuality… By pleasure we mean the absence of pain in the body and of trouble in the soul".
  - 130–131: "bread and water confer the highest possible pleasure when they are brought to hungry lips".
- SEP "Epicurus" (rev. 2022): dates 341–270 BCE; the goal is the absence of physical pain and mental disturbance; the three kinds of desire (LM 127), with immortality as the example of an empty desire.
- SEP "Stoicism": pleasure belongs with things that are "neither good nor bad"; only virtue is good (counterpoint slide).

### filsafat-wu-wei-id
- *Tao Teh King*, tr. James Legge (Gutenberg #216):
  - ch. 37: "The Tao in its regular course does nothing (for the sake of doing it), and so there is nothing which it does not do" (the quote slide).
  - ch. 48: "He diminishes it and again diminishes it, till he arrives at doing nothing (on purpose). Having arrived at this point of non-action, there is nothing which he does not do".
- SEP "Laozi":
  - "Wuwei does not mean total inaction"; translations include "non-coercive action" and "effortless action".
  - Laozi's identity is debated, and the text is composite, consolidated during the 4th c. BCE and stable by the mid-3rd c. BCE.
  - Interpreters read it as spiritual, or as ethics and political philosophy.
- The bicycle example is the deck's own illustration.

### filsafat-jujur-atau-baik-id
- SEP "Kant's Moral Philosophy": the maxim of lying whenever it gets you what you want contradicts itself once universalised; there is a perfect duty not to make lying promises. Kant's 1797 essay (the murderer at the door) isn't cited, because no accessible full text was checked.
- J.S. Mill, *Utilitarianism* (1861) ch. 2 (Gutenberg #11224): veracity is "one of the most useful" things. Its exceptions: "the withholding of some fact (as of information from a male-factor, or of bad news from a person dangerously ill)".
- SEP "Aristotle's Ethics": ethical virtue is complete only with practical wisdom (*phronêsis*), and feelings are to be experienced "in the right way at the right times".

### filsafat-stoik-bukan-mati-rasa-id
- SEP "Stoicism":
  - The four passions rest on a "cognitive mistake" about what is good or bad.
  - The sage's "good feelings" (*eupatheiai*, Diogenes Laertius) refute "the caricature of the Sage as an unfeeling robot".
  - "Pre-emotions" (*propatheiai*, Gellius) are felt even by the sage, because they occur without assent.
- SEP "Aristotle's Ethics": unlike the Stoics, who held anger and fear "always inappropriate", Aristotle thought they should be felt "in the right way at the right times".

### filsafat-konfusius-tahu-id
